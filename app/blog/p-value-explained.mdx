# Hypothesis testing, explained without the math horror

**The p-value is the most misunderstood concept in statistics.**

## What a p-value is NOT

- ❌ Probability your hypothesis is true
- ❌ Probability you made an error
- ❌ A measure of effect size
- ❌ Proof of anything

## What a p-value actually is

A p-value is **the probability of observing data at least as extreme as what you observed, assuming the null hypothesis is true**.

### Example: A/B test

You test a new website design.

- **Null hypothesis (H₀):** New design = Old design
- **Alternative (H₁):** New design ≠ Old design

You compute p = 0.03.

**What this means:** IF there were truly no difference, there's a 3% chance of seeing a difference this big or bigger.

## The threshold (α) is arbitrary

The 0.05 threshold comes from Ronald Fisher's 1925 book. There's no mathematical law that says 5% is the magic number.

## What to report instead

1. **Effect size** — How big is the difference?
2. **Confidence interval** — Range of plausible values
3. **Sample size** — Is this powered enough?
4. **Practical significance** — Does it matter?

## Conclusion

The p-value is a useful tool, not a verdict.
